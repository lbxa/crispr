# Genome Code of Conduct

Genome should be a place where people can contribute, ask questions, and disagree
without being intimidated or demeaned. This applies regardless of background,
identity, disability, experience, or professional status.

## How we work together

- Discuss the work and its evidence. Give specific, useful feedback without
  attacking the person who contributed it.
- Make room for different perspectives and levels of experience. Explain unfamiliar
  concepts, and respect people's names, pronouns, and access needs.
- Respect boundaries. Stop an interaction when asked, and obtain permission before
  sharing someone else's personal information or private correspondence.
- Correct mistakes openly and take responsibility for their impact.
- Handle genetic and health information with particular care. Use sanitized or
  synthetic examples in public discussions, and respect consent and data ownership.

## Unacceptable conduct

Harassment, discrimination, threats, stalking, sexual harassment, deliberate
humiliation, and repeated personal attacks are not acceptable. Neither are exposing
private information, pressuring someone to disclose health or genetic data,
retaliating against a reporter, or evading a moderation restriction.

Technical disagreement and good-faith criticism are welcome. They do not excuse
abusive behavior.

## Where this applies

This code applies to the repository, issues, pull requests, project discussions,
and other spaces managed by Genome maintainers. It also applies when someone
represents Genome at an event or through an official project channel.

## Reporting a concern

Email [privacy@genome.com](mailto:privacy@genome.com) with the subject
"Genome conduct report". Share what happened, where it happened, and any relevant
links or sanitized evidence. You do not need to confront the person involved or
make a public report first.

Do not post private reports, genome files, medical records, credentials, or upload
tokens in an issue. If a report involves a maintainer, say so; that person should
not investigate or decide the outcome of their own case. A report can be made on
behalf of someone else with their permission.

Maintainers will limit access to reports to people needed to investigate and
respond. Information may need to be shared to address a safety concern or meet a
legal obligation; absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. Report handling
is also covered by the [privacy notice](PRIVACY.md).

## Response and enforcement

Maintainers will review reports in context, seek clarification where needed, and
take action proportionate to the conduct and its impact. Possible actions include
asking for a correction, removing content, issuing a warning, restricting
participation temporarily, or banning someone from project spaces. Serious or
repeated harm may warrant immediate restrictions.

Maintainers may moderate harmful content without waiting for a report. Where
appropriate, they will explain the decision to the people affected without
disclosing private report details.

To request reconsideration, reply through the same private address with the
decision and any relevant new information. A maintainer who was not involved in
the incident should review the request when one is available. Retaliation for
reporting a concern or participating in a review is itself a violation.
